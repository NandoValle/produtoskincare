Retinol: how to start without irritating your skin

By the Produto Skincare team · Updated on November 4, 2026

Published on November 2, 2026 · 6-minute read

Retinol belongs to the retinoid family, some of the most studied ingredients against skin aging. The most studied and most potent ones are prescription retinoids, such as tretinoin; retinol is a milder version sold without a prescription (Mukherjee et al., 2006; Sadick et al., 2023). Even so, it can irritate when used the wrong way. Here is how to start right.

Summary: Start with a low concentration (up to 0.3%), use it only at night, twice a week, and only increase after a few weeks without irritation. Less is more.

What retinol does

  • Stimulates collagen: softens fine lines. In a randomized trial with older adults, 0.4% retinol applied to the arm up to 3 times a week for 24 weeks improved fine wrinkles and increased collagen (Kafi et al., 2007)
  • Improves texture and tone: retinoids make the skin smoother and the tone more even (Sadick et al., 2023)

One caveat: for most products sold without a prescription, clinical studies are still few (Sadick et al., 2023).

In general, the higher the concentration, the higher the chance of redness, peeling and burning (Sadick et al., 2023). Retinol and retinal tend to irritate much less than tretinoin (Mukherjee et al., 2006).

A safe progression (4 weeks)

📅 Weeks 1-2: just the basics
  • Gentle cleanser + moisturizer + sunscreen
  • Don't add retinol yet
  • Your skin needs to get used to the routine
📅 Week 3: first contact
  • Use retinol twice a week (e.g. Tuesday and Friday)
  • Only at night, after moisturizer (the "sandwich" technique)
  • Amount: a pea-sized drop for the whole face
📅 Week 4+: increase slowly
  • If there was no irritation, go up to 3 times a week, the maximum frequency in the trial mentioned above (Kafi et al., 2007)
  • After 3 months, if your skin is doing well, consider 0.5%
  • Avoid using it 2 nights in a row at the beginning

Common mistakes (avoid them!)

  • ❌ Using it every day right away: raises the chance of irritation
  • ❌ Using acids (BHA/AHA) on the same night: irritation can add up
  • ❌ Using it in the morning: some retinoids, such as tretinoin, are very sensitive to light (Martin et al., 1998), which is why they are usually used at night
  • ❌ Forgetting sunscreen: the sun is the biggest factor in facial aging, exactly what retinol is trying to treat (Sadick et al., 2023)
  • ❌ Giving up at the first peeling: mild redness, peeling and dryness are common side effects of retinoids (Sadick et al., 2023). Use it less often; if it burns a lot or doesn't improve, stop and see a dermatologist
  • ❌ Using it during pregnancy on your own: a meta-analysis found no increase in birth defects with topical retinoids, but the data are not enough to justify use during pregnancy (Kaplan et al., 2015). Talk to your doctor
