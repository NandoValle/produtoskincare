Myth vs rule: tingling is not a sign that a product is working

The myth

"Feel that burn? It means it is working."

The rule

If it burns, the skin is asking you to stop. Efficacy is proven in weeks — not by pain in the first minute.

Straight answer: If it burns, the skin is asking you to stop — not celebrating. Cosmetic efficacy is proven with studies and in the mirror over weeks, not with pain in the first minute.

The myth

“Feel that burn? That means it's doing its job.” The pain-equals-proof logic became common sense — exactly the kind of common sense that burns the skin barrier.

What dermatology and the rule say

The 2026 wave of reporting points the same way: dermatologists warn that overusing potent actives compromises the skin barrier and can worsen acne, redness and sensitivity — often driven by social media trends. As one dermatologist told O Globo (Nov 5, 2026), a famous ingredient on the label is not enough: concentration, stability and the whole formulation determine what reaches your skin.

And one rule dismantles the pain-as-proof promise: RDC No. 752/2022 (art. 5) obliges the manufacturer to hold proof for label claims — and forbids (art. 12) any therapeutic claim. In short: “it burns because it works” is not a promise cosmetics may make in Brazil.

The rule

ANVISA Resolution RDC No. 752/2022, arts. 5 and 12 — the manufacturer must hold data proving the safety and efficacy of label claims; therapeutic claims (“treats”, “cures”) are forbidden to cosmetics. “It burns because it works” is not a promise Brazilian law allows.

What to watch for — and when to stop

  • Signs to pause: burning that does not fade, persistent redness, heavy peeling, swelling, itching.
  • Introduce one active at a time, on alternate days at first; test on a small area first.
  • Peels and retinoids are not self-medication: professional guidance changes the outcome — and prevents damage.
  • If the skin improves over weeks, without drama, that is the way — consistency beats intensity.

Frequently asked questions

So products that burn are never useful?

Not quite. Some actives can cause mild initial discomfort that fades — but strong or persistent burning is a sign to stop and, if needed, see a dermatologist.

Is a “burn test” reliable for allergies?

No. Allergic reactions can appear days later. Testing on a small area reduces risk but does not replace professional evaluation.

What is the “skin barrier” everyone talks about?

It is the outer layer of your skin, which keeps water in and irritants out. When it is compromised, skin becomes sensitive, red and reactive — the opposite of the result you want.
