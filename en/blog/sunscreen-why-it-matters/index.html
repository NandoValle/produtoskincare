Sunscreen: the most important step in skincare

By the Produto Skincare team · Updated on November 4, 2026

Published on November 2, 2026 · 5-minute read

If you could use only ONE skincare product, sunscreen would be the best bet. Few skincare products have studies as strong as sunscreen does, including a randomized trial that ran for several years (Hughes et al., 2013).

Summary: SPF 30 or higher (many people prefer 50+), every day, even on cloudy days. Apply two fingers' worth of product to your face and reapply every 2 hours in the sun.

Why does it matter so much?

  • About 80% of the visible signs of facial aging were linked to the sun in a study of 298 women (Flament et al., 2013)
  • Dark spots: in the same study, spots were strongly linked to sun exposure (Flament et al., 2013)
  • Skin cancer: in a trial in Australia, the group that applied sunscreen every day had fewer melanomas over the following 10 years (11 versus 22 cases) (Green et al., 2010)
  • Anti-aging: in the same Australian trial, people who used sunscreen every day showed 24% less skin aging over 4.5 years than those who used it now and then (Hughes et al., 2013)

UVA vs UVB

UVAUVB
What it doesMore linked to aging (spots, wrinkles)Main cause of sunburn (redness)
Goes through glass?✅ Partly, depending on the glass❌ Almost none
Where to protectAlways, including near windowsOutdoors

Ordinary window glass and car glass block almost all UVB, but some UVA gets through, depending on the type of glass (Almutawa et al., 2013).

Look for "broad spectrum" or "UVA/UVB" on the label: it protects against both.

How much to apply?

  • Face: 2 index fingers' length (≈ 1/4 teaspoon)
  • Face + neck: 4 fingers
  • Body: 30 ml (about a shot glass)

The SPF on the label is measured with a layer of 2 mg per cm² of skin. At the beach, people usually apply only about a quarter of that, and protection drops much more than by half: with half the amount, an SPF 50 delivers something close to SPF 7 (Faurschou et al., 2007).

When to reapply?

  • Every 2 hours of sun exposure (Diffey et al., 2023)
  • After sweating or swimming
  • After drying your face with a towel
  • If you spend the day near a window: it is worth reapplying at midday, because some UVA goes through glass (Almutawa et al., 2013)

Common mistakes

  • ❌ "It's cloudy, I don't need it": clouds don't block all UV radiation, and the benefit in studies came from daily use (Hughes et al., 2013)
  • ❌ "I only use it in summer": what showed results was using it every day, not now and then (Hughes et al., 2013)
  • ❌ "My moisturizer already has SPF": in one study, people who applied a moisturizer with SPF left more areas of the face uncovered, especially the eyelids, than with sunscreen (Lourenco et al., 2019)
  • ❌ "I apply it once and it lasts all day": outdoors, you need to reapply (Diffey et al., 2023)
  • ❌ "I don't use it indoors": if you sit near a window, some UVA goes through the glass (Almutawa et al., 2013)
Reminder: Sunscreen is the last step of the morning routine (after cleansing, treatment and moisturizer).

Frequently asked questions

How much sunscreen should I apply to my face?

The page says 2 index fingers' length for the face (about 1/4 teaspoon) and 4 fingers for face and neck. With half the amount, an SPF 50 delivers something close to SPF 7, so applying too little costs a lot of protection.

How often should I reapply sunscreen?

Every 2 hours of sun exposure, and after sweating, swimming or drying your face with a towel. If you spend the day near a window, reapplying at midday is worthwhile, because some UVA goes through glass.

What does broad spectrum mean on a sunscreen label?

Broad spectrum, or UVA/UVB on the label, means the sunscreen protects against both. UVA is more linked to aging (spots, wrinkles), while UVB is the main cause of sunburn.
