Vitamin C: what it does and how to use it

By the Produto Skincare team · Updated on November 4, 2026

Published on November 2, 2026 · 5-minute read

Vitamin C is one of the most popular skincare ingredients. It is an antioxidant, plays a part in collagen production and may help with dark spots (Badash et al., 2026). Clinical studies are still limited (Al-Niaimi et al., 2017), and using it the wrong way can waste the product (or even irritate your skin).

Summary: Use it preferably in the morning, before sunscreen. Start with 10%. Store it somewhere dark and cool.

What vitamin C does

  • Brightens the tone: helps make skin tone more even (Badash et al., 2026)
  • Helps with dark spots: reduces melanin formation (Al-Niaimi et al., 2017). In melasma, a trial with 5% vitamin C lightened less than hydroquinone, but with almost no side effects (Espinal-Perez et al., 2004)
  • Antioxidant: helps protect the skin from environmental damage, especially from the sun (Badash et al., 2026)
  • Complements sunscreen: it works through a different pathway from UV filters, but does not replace SPF (Murray et al., 2008)
  • Supports collagen: increases production and reduces breakdown, which may soften fine lines (Al-Niaimi et al., 2017)

When to use it: preferably in the morning

Most people use it in the morning, because it:

  • Protects against the day's free radicals
  • Adds to the protection of sunscreen (Murray et al., 2008)

But using it at night is not a waste. In a study on pig skin, vitamin C stayed in the skin for days after application (Pinnell et al., 2001), and a melasma trial used the product at night (Espinal-Perez et al., 2004).

Ideal concentration

ConcentrationFor whomWhat we know
5-10%Beginners, sensitive skinMilder. In the 5% trial, there were almost no side effects (Espinal-Perez et al., 2004)
10-15%Most peopleA common range. A study in people used 15%, together with vitamin E and ferulic acid (Murray et al., 2008)
15-20%Experienced usersIn pig skin, 20% was the concentration with the best absorption (Pinnell et al., 2001)
Above 20%—In the same study, it did not increase absorption

How to use it (step by step)

  1. Cleanse your face
  2. Apply vitamin C to dry skin (3-5 drops)
  3. Wait 1-2 minutes for it to absorb
  4. Apply moisturizer
  5. Finish with sunscreen (a must!)

Combinations that work

CombinationResult
Vitamin C + sunscreen✅ Each complements the other (Murray et al., 2008)
Vitamin C + vitamin E✅ They work together against oxidation (Al-Niaimi et al., 2017)
Vitamin C + ferulic acid✅ A more stable formula; with vitamin E, it protected the skin against UV damage in a study in people (Murray et al., 2008)
Vitamin C + niacinamide⚠️ Studies on the pair are lacking; if it stings, use them at different times of day
Vitamin C + retinol⚠️ Together they may irritate more; the simplest approach is vitamin C in the morning and retinol at night

Frequently asked questions

Should I use vitamin C in the morning or at night?

Preferably in the morning, since most people use it then to protect against the day's free radicals and add to sunscreen. Using it at night is not a waste: in a pig skin study, vitamin C stayed in the skin for days after application.

What does antioxidant mean for vitamin C in skincare?

Antioxidant means it helps protect the skin from environmental damage, especially from the sun. Vitamin C works through a different pathway from UV filters, so it complements sunscreen but does not replace SPF.

Can I use vitamin C together with retinol?

Together they may irritate more. The simplest approach is vitamin C in the morning and retinol at night. For vitamin C with niacinamide, studies are lacking; if it stings, use them at different times of day.
