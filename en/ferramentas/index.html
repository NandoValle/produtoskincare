When the results show up

The most expensive mistake in skincare is not picking the wrong product — it is quitting in the wrong week. Every active has its own pace: some show up in days, others only after three months. Choose the actives in your routine and see the honest calendar of what to expect, week by week.

What this calendar is (and is not)

The time ranges are estimates for managing expectations, in the format of common dermatology practice guidance. They are not a promise, not a prescription, and they vary with concentration, frequency, skin type and consistency. The site's fixed reference is the 12-Week Mode: wait at least 6 weeks before judging the result.

The most expensive mistake in skincare

Quitting in week 3. The money for the bottle is already spent; the result is not. Retinol and acids usually go through an adjustment phase (peeling, purging, more sensitive skin) right when there is still no visible result: that is the zone where most people stop, and it is where the calendar above shows the math starting to close.

Two costs, one math

The cost per day shows how much the product weighs on your wallet. This calendar shows how much it weighs on your patience: a R$ 100 retinol abandoned in week 3 costs dearly per day of use and returns zero result. The deal is simple: start slow (the 12-Week Mode plays that role), protect from the sun every day, and only judge after 6 weeks.

In short

  • Hydration changes in days. Oiliness and texture take weeks. Spots and lines take months.
  • The first 2 to 4 weeks of a new active are the adjustment phase: the result is almost never there.
  • Site rule: wait at least 6 weeks before judging. If 3 months pass with no change at all, it is worth the conversation with a dermatologist.
  • Consistency beats potency: the product you use every day wins over the perfect product you abandon.
